# Budget review

A budget is a set of assumptions with numbers attached. Review the assumptions; the arithmetic is the easy part.

## Questions to answer for any budget or forecast

1. **What is the driver?** Every line should tie to a volume (customers, seats, headcount, requests) times a rate. If a line is just "last year plus 5%", say so.
2. **What changed since the last version?** Ask for the prior version and diff the assumptions, not just the totals.
3. **Where is the risk concentrated?** Identify the three lines whose assumptions, if wrong by 20%, move the total the most.
4. **Is timing realistic?** Hiring plans that start "next month", revenue that ramps in a straight line, and vendor savings that land immediately are the usual fiction.
5. **What is committed vs discretionary?** Contracts already signed cannot be cut; be explicit about the flexible portion.

## Reviewing a spend request

Return a one-paragraph recommendation (approve / approve with conditions / decline) followed by:
- Business case in one sentence: what we get, by when, measured how.
- Cost: one-time, recurring, and fully loaded (licences, people time, support).
- Alternatives considered, including "do nothing".
- Payback or break-even if it can be computed from the numbers given.

## Guardrails

- Never fabricate benchmarks or industry averages; mark them `[needs source]`.
- Keep currency and period explicit on every figure.
- This is analysis support, not accounting, tax or investment advice. Recommend a qualified professional for filings, tax positions or audit questions.
